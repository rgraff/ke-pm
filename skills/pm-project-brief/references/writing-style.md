# House writing style

> Source: kalkomey/playbook — `standards/project-name-and-briefs.md`
> (as of 2026-08-15)

Applies to everything `ke-pm` writes into ClickUp.

## The rules

- **Grade 9 reading level.** Write like you are explaining this to a smart
  freshman in high school.
- **Plain words.** If a simpler word works, use it.
- **Short sentences.** One idea per sentence.
- **No jargon or business speak.**

## Banned words

Never publish these: **leverage, synergy, operationalize, stakeholders, robust,
utilize**.

Also cut filler: "it is important to note", "in order to achieve", "at the end
of the day", "moving forward".

Replacements that usually work: leverage → use. Utilize → use. Operationalize →
run, or set up. Robust → sturdy, or reliable, or just delete it. Stakeholders →
name the actual people or teams.

## The style pass

After drafting, read the Brief back and fix:

1. **Any banned word or filler phrase.**
2. **Sentences carrying two ideas.** Split them.
3. **Solution language in `Problem`.** That section says what is broken or
   missing. If a sentence describes what you are going to build, it belongs in
   `Approach`.
4. **Abstraction covering a gap.** "Improve the customer experience" usually
   means nobody said what is wrong. Go back and ask rather than publishing the
   vagueness.
5. **Length.** If `Summary` does not fit above the fold, cut it down.

## The tone to avoid

The failure mode is a Brief that reads like it was written to be seen rather
than read — polished, confident, and empty. That is performative thoroughness,
and it is worse than a rough Brief because it hides what nobody knows yet.

Prefer a plain sentence that admits uncertainty over a smooth one that implies
it does not exist. "We do not yet know which of the two checkout flows converts
better" beats "We will optimise the conversion funnel."

## You own the result

The user is responsible for what gets published under their name. Show them the
draft, take their line edits, and do not defend your phrasing. If they correct
something as inaccurate, fix it — you are drafting, not authoring.
